Benefit #1

Down payment, with full entitlement.

The VA guarantees a portion of your loan. With your entitlement intact, you can buy a primary residence — even a multi-million-dollar one — without putting a dollar down.

Optional: putting 5%+ down drops your funding fee. We'll get to the math in a minute.

No PMI · No MIP
1Eligibility

Service requirements, plainly.

You qualify if you meet one of these minimums. The Certificate of Eligibility (COE) is the official document that proves it — a competent lender pulls it same-day.

★

Active Duty

90 continuous days of active service during wartime, or 181 days during peacetime.

⚐

National Guard / Reserves

6 years of service, or 90 days of active-duty service (Title 10 or 32).

✚

Veterans

Discharge under any condition other than dishonorable after meeting the minimums above.

♡

Surviving Spouses

Un-remarried spouses of service members who died in the line of duty or from a service-connected disability.

Teaching note: "Active service" means full-time orders. Inactive duty for training (drills) doesn't count toward the minimums.
2Entitlement

What entitlement actually means.

"Entitlement" is the dollar amount the VA promises to back on your loan. It's the most misunderstood concept in the program — and the one that unlocks $0 down on any price.

The VA guarantees 25% of your loan to the lender. That guaranty is why lenders are willing to lend with no down payment — they're already protected on the first 25%.

What entitlement gives you
  • $0 down on a primary residence, at any price your income supports
  • No private mortgage insurance — ever
  • The right to use it again — and again — for life
  • Two active VA loans at once if you have partial entitlement left

How a $500,000 VA loan is backed

The VA covers the first 25% of the loan amount. Your lender takes the rest.

VA Guaranty · 25% · $125,000
Lender's exposure · 75% · $375,000
Why your lender lends with $0 down →
Full vs. partial entitlement

Full entitlement

You've never used your benefit, or you've fully restored it after paying off a prior VA loan. No loan limit applies. Buy a $2M house with $0 down if the lender approves the income.

Partial entitlement

You have an active VA loan or unrestored entitlement. The county conforming loan limit (~$806,500 for most counties in 2026) caps the no-down-payment amount; you can still buy above it with a down payment on the excess.

Worked Example · Partial Entitlement

Buying with a second VA loan

An active-duty captain is PCS'ing to San Diego. She kept her Norfolk home (renting it out, still has the VA loan on it) and wants to buy in San Diego at $700,000. Partial entitlement applies.

San Diego County 2026 conforming limit$1,209,750
Entitlement used on Norfolk loan (guaranty)$70,000
Remaining guaranty available$232,437
Max purchase with $0 down$929,750

She's buying at $700,000 — comfortably under the cap. Zero down, two VA loans active at once.

Worked Example · The Real Cost

$400,000 purchase, first-time use, $0 down

Most veterans never actually feel this fee — it's added to the loan balance and amortized over 30 years. Here's what that looks like.

You pay $0 funding fee if any apply
  • ✓

    VA disability compensation

    You're receiving compensation for a service-connected disability.

  • ✓

    Eligible but receiving retired/active-duty pay

    You qualify for disability comp but take retirement or active-duty pay instead.

  • ✓

    Surviving spouse with DIC

  • ✓

Watch out: If your disability rating comes through after closing, you can apply for a refund of the funding fee. Most veterans don't know to ask.
4Credit

The VA sets no minimum. Lenders do.

Most lenders impose their own overlay around 580–620. The VA itself cares about your overall pattern, residual income, and how recent any setbacks were — not a single number.

The VA doesn't have a minimum credit score. Anyone who tells you "you need a 620" is quoting their lender's rule — not the program's.

VA Lender's Handbook, Chapter 4
Move your score fast
  • Pay every revolving account down below 30% utilization (under 10% is better)
  • Don't close old accounts — length of history matters
  • Dispute inaccurate items in writing, with documentation
  • Avoid new credit pulls in the 90 days before applying
  • Ask for a rapid rescore if you've paid down debt — your LO can do this in 3–5 days
What underwriters actually look at
12

The last 12 months

Recent on-time payments matter far more than old delinquencies. Twelve clean months is the working benchmark.

$

Collections & charge-offs

Medical collections are routinely ignored. Non-medical collections may need to be paid or explained.

▲

Trend, not score

A 600 score that's rising beats a 660 that's falling. Underwriters look at the direction.

5DTI & Residual Income

The VA does something no other loan does.

It pairs DTI with residual income — the cash you actually have left over each month after taxes, housing, debts, and basic living costs. A 50%+ DTI can pass if residual is strong.

The residual income equation
  1. Start with gross monthly income
  2. Subtract federal, state, and Social Security taxes
  3. Subtract your full housing payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance)
  4. Subtract minimum payments on every other debt
  5. Subtract a maintenance & utilities allowance (~14¢/sq ft per month)
  6. What's left is your residual income — it must clear the regional minimum
%

DTI Benchmark

41% is the VA's guideline — not a hard cap. Higher is fine if residual income is solid.

$

Residual Income

Gross income minus federal/state/SS taxes, minus housing, debts, and living. Must clear regional minimums.

⌂

Family Size Scales

Required residual scales with household size — more people, more required cushion.

+

Compensating Factors

Strong reserves, long job tenure, low utilization — all push higher DTIs through underwriting.

7Property Requirements

What you can buy — and what the VA will reject.

Two checks: the property type has to be eligible, and the condition has to clear the VA's Minimum Property Requirements (MPRs).

What the VA will never finance
  • Investment properties — primary residence only
  • Co-ops (yes, even in NYC)
  • Working farms and commercial buildings
  • Vacation or second homes
  • Bare land with no plans to build immediately
Property types allowed
  • ✓

    Single-family

    Primary residence only. No investment properties.

  • ✓

    2–4 unit multifamily

    You must occupy one unit. Rental income from the others can help qualify.

  • ✓

    VA-approved condos

    The condo project itself must be on the VA-approved list.

  • ✓

    Manufactured homes

    Must be permanently affixed to a foundation on owned land.

  • ✓

    New construction

    VA-builder warranty required; phased inspections during build.

Common MPR flags
  • ✕

    Roof at end of life

    Less than 3 years of useful life remaining — fail.

  • ✕

    Active wood-destroying pests

    Termite report required in most states; active infestation must be cured.

  • ✕

    No working HVAC

    The home must be heatable to a livable temperature.

  • ✕

    Peeling paint (pre-1978)

    Lead-based-paint hazard. Must be scraped, primed, and repainted.

  • ✕

    Standing water under house

    Crawl-space drainage issues are a common kill.

How to win on a fixer-upper: Sellers can complete required repairs before close (most common), or price-in an escrow holdback. Knowing which to push for is what an experienced VA agent earns.
8Appraisal & Tidewater

The VA appraisal, and the Tidewater process.

VA appraisals get a bad reputation. They shouldn't. The Tidewater process actually gives buyers a heads-up no other loan offers.

1

Lender orders

Appraisal ordered through the VA portal within a few days of contract. The VA assigns the appraiser — not the lender.

2

Tidewater notice

If the appraiser thinks value will come in low, they notify the listing agent before finalizing.

3

Comps submitted

Listing agent has 48 hours to submit supporting comps and any improvements not visible on the MLS.

4

Notice of Value

Appraiser issues the NOV. If low, buyer can renegotiate, pay the gap in cash, or walk per contract.

Why veterans should want this: Tidewater is a free second opinion on whether the seller's asking price is defensible. Cash buyers don't get this. Conventional buyers don't get this.
If the appraisal comes in low
  • Renegotiate the contract price down to the appraised value
  • Pay the gap in cash at closing (funds must be documented)
  • Request a Reconsideration of Value with new comps your agent finds
  • Walk away — your earnest money is protected by the VA appraisal contingency
9Closing Costs & Seller Concessions

How veterans close with $0 out of pocket.

Typical closing costs run 2–5% of the purchase price. With the right offer structure, the seller pays all of it — and the funding fee, and your prepaids, and your debts.

What "100% seller-paid closing" really covers
  • Lender origination, processing, and underwriting fees
  • Title insurance — both lender's and owner's
  • Escrow and recording fees
  • VA appraisal fee
  • Transfer taxes (where applicable)
  • Property tax and homeowners insurance escrow setup
  • Plus 4% concessions: funding fee, debt payoff, prepaids, points
Typical closing-cost buckets
$

Lender fees

Origination, processing, underwriting. The VA caps origination at 1% of the loan amount.

⌂

Appraisal & inspection

VA appraisal: $600–$900. Home inspection: $400–$700.

📄

Title & escrow

Title insurance (lender's + owner's), escrow fees, recording, transfer taxes.

🏛

Prepaid escrow

Property taxes (6–12 months) + homeowners insurance (12 months) collected up front.

What the seller can pay

Allowable closing costs

The seller can pay 100% of your allowable closing costs — no percentage cap. Origination, title, appraisal, recording, all of it.

Concessions (4% cap)

On top of closing costs, up to 4% of the loan amount in concessions: funding fee, debt payoff, prepaids, points to buy down the rate.

Worked Example · The Seller-Credit Play

$0 cash to close on a $450,000 purchase

Buyer is a first-time VA user, no exemption. List was $462K; buyer offered $450K with $14K in seller credits. Here's how the math lands.

Purchase price (negotiated up from list)$465,000
Loan amount before funding fee$465,000
Funding fee (2.15%)$10,000
Total financed$475,000
Estimated closing costs (2.8%)$13,000
Prepaid escrow (taxes + insurance)$5,500
Seller credit (negotiated into price)$18,500
Cash to close$0

Veteran walks into a home with no money down, no money at the table, and a slightly higher payment (~$90/mo). Often a better trade than depleting savings.

10Rates & Buydowns

Why VA rates are typically lower.

VA loans price 0.25%–0.50% below conventional, on average. The reason: the VA guaranty reduces lender risk, so they price the loan more aggressively.

Why VA rates run lower
  • Lender risk is capped — the VA guaranty backs the first 25%
  • The MBS market treats VA loans as premium collateral — they trade tighter
  • Servicing is cleaner — VA borrowers default at lower rates than conventional
  • You don't need stellar credit for the best pricing — score buckets are flatter
Three ways to lower your rate further
↓

Permanent buydown (points)

Pay 1% of the loan amount up front to drop the rate by roughly 0.25% for the life of the loan. Seller can cover this with concessions.

2-1

Temporary buydown (2-1)

Rate is 2% lower in year 1, 1% lower in year 2, then back to the note rate. Funded by the seller — buyer keeps the savings.

⟲

IRRRL when rates drop

The Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan — streamlined refi, no appraisal, no income docs. Cheapest path back into a lower rate.

11VA Loan Assumption

Your low rate is a marketing tool.

VA loans are assumable. When you sell, a qualified buyer can take over your existing loan — and your existing rate. In a high-rate market, that's a six-figure feature.

Why this matters

If you locked a 3.25% VA loan in 2021 and the market rate is 7.25% when you sell, a buyer assuming your loan saves roughly $1,200/month on a $400K balance. Your listing agent can ask a premium.

The catch

Your entitlement stays tied to the loan until it's paid off. If the buyer isn't a veteran, you can't restore entitlement and use it for your next home. Veteran-to-veteran assumptions are the cleanest path.

14Timeline

From offer to keys: a 30-day path.

A clean VA file closes in 28–35 days. Below is a typical timeline once you're under contract.

Day0

Mutual acceptance

Offer accepted, contract signed. Earnest money wired within 2–3 business days. Lender opens the file and orders the VA appraisal.

Day3

Disclosures & intent to proceed

You receive the Loan Estimate. Sign disclosures and indicate intent to proceed within 10 days of application.

Day7

Home inspection

Independent of the VA appraisal — your tool to catch issues. Negotiate repairs or price reduction during the inspection period.

Day14

VA appraisal back

Notice of Value issued. Any MPR flags get communicated to the listing agent for repair before closing.

Day21

Conditional approval

Underwriting issues conditional approval. Final docs requested — updated pay stubs, bank statements, any explanation letters.

Day26

Clear to close

All conditions satisfied. The Closing Disclosure is issued — by law, you have 3 business days to review before signing.

Day30

Closing & keys

Sign at the title company or escrow office. Funds wire. Recording happens same- or next-day. The home is yours.

Reference

Acronyms & terms, decoded.

Every VA acronym you'll meet, in one place. Bookmark this section.

COECertificate of Eligibility
The document confirming you've met service requirements. Pulled by your lender in minutes via the VA portal.
DTIDebt-to-Income Ratio
Your monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income. The VA benchmark is 41%, with residual income as the secondary check.
MPRMinimum Property Requirements
The VA's habitability standard. Covers roof, HVAC, water, pests, lead paint, and structural soundness.
NOVNotice of Value
The final document from the VA appraiser stating the home's appraised value and any required repairs.
BAHBasic Allowance for Housing
Active-duty housing allowance, set by duty station and rank. Counts as qualifying income for VA loans.
LESLeave and Earnings Statement
Active-duty monthly pay statement. Required income documentation for active-duty borrowers.
IRRRLInterest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan
The VA's "streamline refi" — no appraisal, no income docs, lower rate. Pronounced "earl."
DICDependency & Indemnity Compensation
Monthly tax-free benefit paid to surviving spouses of service members who died in service or from a service-connected disability.
PITIPrincipal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance
The four components of your monthly mortgage payment. The "true" cost of housing for DTI calculations.
LTVLoan-to-Value
Loan amount divided by appraised value. VA loans routinely go to 100% LTV (or higher with the funding fee rolled in).
UFMIPUp-Front Mortgage Insurance Premium
FHA's equivalent of the funding fee. Charged on every FHA loan. The VA's funding fee is one-time; FHA also charges monthly MIP on top.
TidewaterTidewater Initiative
Pre-appraisal value challenge process. If the appraiser believes value will come in low, the listing side gets 48 hours to defend the price.
